Lamp

Light as material

A study in how form becomes inseparable from the quality of light it produces. The geometry is generated parametrically to control wall thickness, which in turn controls how light diffuses through the printed shell — thinner walls glow, thicker walls shadow.

The print material acts as a diffuser. Each layer contributes to a gradient of translucency from base to crown, shifting the perceived colour temperature of the light source as the viewing angle changes.

Material as Optics

Standard FDM printing at 0.2mm layer height produces a diffusion profile that closely approximates a Gaussian spread at thin wall sections. The outer surface is left matte to scatter ambient light, while the interior is smooth to maximize internal reflection toward the aperture.

Process

Form is derived from the light distribution target rather than aesthetic preference. A desired lux curve at 1m is specified, then the wall profile is iterated until the physical print matches the target within tolerance.
